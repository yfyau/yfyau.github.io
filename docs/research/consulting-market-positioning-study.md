# CR-001: Consulting market and search-positioning study

- **Date:** 2026-08-28
- **Status:** Recommendation-ready; no website implementation yet
- **Initial market:** Canada, with Ontario/Toronto and remote North American work as hypotheses to validate
- **Decision question:** Who is most likely to pay Jason for software or systems consulting, what should he offer them, and how should the site support both conventional and AI-assisted discovery?

## Recommendation in one sentence

Do not position Jason as a broad **IT consultant** or a generic **AI consultant**. Position him as a senior software engineer who takes selective consulting engagements for two concrete outcomes: **stabilizing live software products** and **removing costly manual work by connecting and automating business systems**.

This is narrower than the available market, but it matches Jason's public evidence and avoids competing with managed IT providers, web-design shops, and no-code automation agencies on their terms.

## What the data says

### There is a large small-business market, but most buyers are very small

ISED reports 1.10 million Canadian employer businesses as of December 2024; 98.2% were small businesses, and 77.3% had only 1-9 employees. Small businesses are the market, but micro-businesses often have limited budget and little implementation capacity. The initial offer should therefore favour firms large enough to have recurring workflows, multiple tools, and an owner or operator who can sponsor a project rather than targeting every local business.

Source: [ISED, Key Small Business Statistics 2025](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/sme-research-statistics/en/key-small-business-statistics/key-small-business-statistics-2025).

### Buyers report an implementation gap, not just a technology-awareness gap

CFIB's 2025 survey of Canadian SMEs found the leading barriers to implementing technology were lack of knowledge or skills (51%), lack of time to explore options (49%), setup and investment cost (48%), and difficulty finding a solution suited to the business (43%). Twenty-six percent also reported difficulty integrating with current tools. These are good conditions for a bounded senior consultant who can diagnose, select, integrate, build, and hand over one useful result.

Among respondents who could quantify returns, the report found meaningful productivity gains, but this should be treated as survey evidence rather than a promise Jason can repeat for his own engagements.

Source: [CFIB, Digital Transformation: How small businesses in Canada are leveraging AI and technology for growth and productivity](https://www.cfib-fcei.ca/hubfs/research/reports/2025/SMEs%20Digital%20transformation%20journey%202025-EN.pdf).

### AI demand is growing, but an AI-first headline would overstate what most firms are buying

Statistics Canada found that 12.2% of businesses used AI to produce goods or deliver services in the 12 months leading into Q2 2025, up from 6.1% a year earlier. Adoption was higher in information and cultural industries (35.6%), professional/scientific/technical services (31.7%), and finance and insurance (30.6%). Common applications included text analytics, data analytics, virtual agents/chatbots, and marketing automation.

In Q3 2025, 14.5% of businesses planned to use AI over the following year, while 66.7% did not. Among non-adopters, 78.1% said AI was not relevant to their goods or services. The signal is to sell a business outcome and use AI only when it is the right implementation tool.

Sources: [Statistics Canada, AI use by businesses, Q2 2025](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2025008-eng.htm) and [Statistics Canada, expected AI use, Q3 2025](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2025011-eng.htm).

### Contract technical work is real, and Jason's public skills align with it

Upwork's US-platform data is not a Canadian market-size estimate, but it is a useful demand signal: its 2025 report placed full-stack development first, mobile app development fourth, and scripting and automation eighth among its most in-demand coding and web-development skills. Scripting and automation was also the fastest-growing skill in that category. The report says 41% of surveyed full-time workers using freelancers turned to them for technical skills.

Source: [Upwork Research Institute, Most In-Demand Skills for 2025](https://www.upwork.com/research/in-demand-skills-2025).

### Security consulting is a real market, but it should not be the initial public offer

Statistics Canada found consultant and contractor expenses were a major component of Canadian business cybersecurity spending in 2023. However, public identity/security consulting would create a higher proof, liability, and possible employer-overlap bar. It should not be marketed until Jason has checked Okta's outside-work, intellectual-property, confidentiality, and conflict-of-interest rules and decided what work he is comfortable taking.

Source: [Statistics Canada, Impact of cybercrime on Canadian businesses, 2023](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/241021/dq241021a-eng.htm).

### Initial industry emphasis

For operations consulting, lead with **professional services and real-estate-adjacent operations**, not every SME sector. These groups are numerous, service-heavy, and close to Jason's prior product context. Finance-adjacent workflows may also fit, but public copy must not imply regulatory, security, or financial-advice credentials that are not being offered.

Construction, retail, transportation, clinics, and hospitality also show digital need, but targeting all of them now would make the page generic. They are later experiments only if referrals or real search queries appear.

## What not to target initially

- **Generic IT support or managed services:** search results are dominated by Microsoft 365, devices, networking, cybersecurity, helpdesk, and ongoing support. That is a different operating model from Jason's software/product strength.
- **Generic website development:** highly commoditized and weakly connected to the strongest public proof.
- **"AI transformation" as a standalone promise:** too broad, difficult to verify, and mismatched with the practical adoption data.
- **24/7 incident response or open-ended maintenance:** creates availability and operational commitments that do not fit selective side engagements.
- **Identity, security, compliance, or Okta implementation:** do not market until employer and conflict boundaries are explicit and the offer has independent evidence.
- **Fractional CTO:** implies company strategy, hiring, budgeting, and leadership ownership beyond the evidence currently presented. A senior hands-on consulting position is more credible.

## Recommended offer architecture

### 1. Systems Reliability Review

For a team with a real product and a recurring operational or delivery problem.

- Inspect one bounded system, incident pattern, performance bottleneck, async workflow, observability gap, or CI/delivery issue.
- Deliver a prioritized diagnosis, concrete next steps, and an implementation option where the scope is suitable.
- Keep the engagement narrow enough that the buyer can judge the result.

This is Jason's most differentiated offer, although it will convert primarily through reputation and referrals rather than broad search volume.

### 2. Workflow Automation Sprint

For an operations-heavy business losing time to repeated work across existing tools.

- Map one workflow and identify the expensive handoffs, duplication, and exceptions.
- Recommend whether to simplify, integrate existing software, add a small custom tool, use deterministic automation, or use AI.
- Build one valuable path with logging, exception handling, documentation, and handoff rather than launching a vague transformation program.

This is the more search-friendly offer, but the market is crowded. Jason's differentiation should be production engineering discipline: reliable integrations, observable failure handling, and ownership after the demo.

### 3. Product Rescue Engagement

For a web or mobile team that needs temporary senior hands-on help to stabilize or ship one difficult area. Keep this as a selective secondary line rather than a full staff-augmentation catalogue.

## Site-positioning recommendation

Keep the home page career-first. Add a compact **Consulting** section immediately **before Contact**, not after Contact. Contact should remain the closing action.

The home-page section should do only four things:

1. Say that Jason takes a limited number of consulting engagements.
2. Name the two problems: live software that needs to hold up, and manual workflows that need to connect.
3. Identify the buyers: product teams and operations-heavy businesses.
4. Offer one direct action: describe the problem by email.

For search discovery, the compact section should link to a substantive `/consulting/` page. That page can explain fit, the two primary offers, engagement shape, relevant proof, exclusions, and contact. It should not grow into separate service pages until real Search Console queries, referrals, or engagements justify them.

### Copy hypothesis, not final copy

> **Selective consulting for difficult systems.**
>
> I help product teams stabilize software that is hard to trust, and help operations-heavy businesses replace repeated manual work with reliable integrations and automation.
>
> Tell me what is breaking, repeating, or blocking delivery.

This is deliberately outcome-led. `IT consultant`, `AI expert`, and long technology lists can appear only where they clarify a real service, not as the main identity.

## Search and AI-discovery strategy

### Search-intent hypotheses to validate

These are content hypotheses, not measured keyword volumes:

- `workflow automation consultant Toronto` / `business process automation consultant Canada`
- `software systems integration consultant`
- `software reliability consultant` / `SaaS reliability consultant`
- `full-stack software consultant` / `mobile app performance consultant`
- `senior software engineer consultant` for selective product rescue

Do not force all variants into one page. Write naturally around buyer problems, location and remote service area only where true, and concrete outcomes. Before creating more pages, validate the wording with Google Ads Keyword Planner or another reputable volume source, then use live Search Console impressions as the better site-specific signal.

### Conventional SEO fundamentals

- Give `/consulting/` its own title, description, canonical URL, visible H1, and internally linked text.
- Keep core content in crawlable HTML and ensure direct page loads return HTTP 200 on the static host.
- Add the page to the sitemap and keep robots access open.
- Extend the existing `Person` entity only with facts visible on the page; use accurate `Service`/offer relationships only if the implementation remains truthful and testable.
- Publish one or two original, consent-safe case notes when evidence exists. A detailed account of the problem, constraints, intervention, and result is more useful than generic AI-written articles.
- Avoid location stuffing, fabricated testimonials, unsupported metrics, and mass-generated service/location pages.

Google's current guidance says the same SEO fundamentals apply to AI Overviews and AI Mode, that structured data must match visible content, and that no special AI schema, rewriting style, content chunking, or `llms.txt` file is needed for Google visibility.

Source: [Google Search Central, Optimizing your website for generative AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).

### ChatGPT and other AI-assisted discovery

The current `robots.txt` uses `User-agent: *` with `Allow: /`, so it does not intentionally block OAI-SearchBot. When a consulting page exists, verify the deployed host and CDN still return it to the crawler and do not add a conflicting bot rule. OpenAI says allowing OAI-SearchBot makes public content eligible for summaries/snippets in ChatGPT search; ranking is not guaranteed. GPTBot training access is a separate choice.

Source: [OpenAI, Publishers and Developers FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq).

Clear entity facts, direct service descriptions, corroborating LinkedIn/GitHub profiles, and original case evidence help both humans and retrieval systems understand what Jason actually does. They are more defensible than an invented "GEO" layer.

## Useful automation after launch

Automate verification and measurement, not content volume:

- On build: assert consulting canonical/title/description, visible H1, structured-data consistency, sitemap inclusion, and absence of accidental `noindex`.
- After deploy: check HTTP 200, canonical host, crawler access, and the direct `/consulting/` URL.
- Submit and monitor through Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools; consider IndexNow for changed consulting URLs if the deployment model makes it useful.
- Review Search Console queries monthly and cluster them by buyer intent, not just traffic.
- Track the consulting email CTA and referral sources. OpenAI documents `utm_source=chatgpt.com` on ChatGPT search referral links, which provides a practical AI-discovery signal.
- Keep a small research queue of real buyer questions and publish only answers where Jason can add firsthand expertise.

Do not automatically scrape search engines at scale or auto-publish keyword pages. That would add operational and quality risk before the site has any conversion evidence.

## Validation plan before design implementation

1. Confirm Jason's allowed engagement boundary under his employment agreement and internal policy.
2. Confirm geography and availability: Toronto/GTA, Canada remote, North America remote, or another explicit scope.
3. Interview or message five plausible buyers/referrers: two product leaders, two SME owner/operators, and one agency lead. Ask about the last problem they paid an external engineer to solve, buying trigger, project size, and objections.
4. Run a small keyword-volume check for the hypotheses above; do not infer demand from a search-results scan alone.
5. Choose one primary offer and one secondary offer, then prototype the home section plus consulting page.
6. Measure qualified conversations, not raw visits, for the first 60-90 days.

## Research limits

- This is a market-direction study, not a total-addressable-market or pricing study.
- Search-result review was qualitative. No Google Ads Keyword Planner, paid SEO database, or Search Console account data was available, so no keyword-volume claim is made.
- Upwork data reflects completed US-platform work and executive surveys; it supports skill demand, not Canadian pricing or conversion.
- The Toronto/local assumption is inferred from the working context and must be confirmed before public copy uses it.
- No conclusion here authorizes an external listing, business registration, analytics account, DNS change, site edit, or deployment.
