# RS-001: Mature personal-site reference study

- **Date:** 2026-08-12
- **Status:** Complete, awaiting direction review
- **Decision question:** Which mature personal-site patterns should guide the next yfyau.com iteration without turning it into a portfolio template, a content business, or an interaction demo?

## Bottom line

No single reference is the right template. The strongest direction for yfyau.com is a **career dossier with one living emblem**:

- use [Brittany Chiang](https://brittanychiang.com/) for career hierarchy and explicit chronology;
- use [Lee Robinson](https://leerob.com/) for editing discipline and direct links;
- use [Maggie Appleton](https://maggieappleton.com/) for authored typography and an unmistakable point of view;
- use [Rauno Freiberg](https://rauno.me/) and [Josh Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/about-josh/) only as permission to make one signature moment memorable, not as permission to turn every section into an interaction.

The current Revision 53 is already close to this hybrid on desktop. The next design problem is narrower: preserve the generated bee and editorial identity while letting mobile visitors reach one credible proof point or direct action earlier.

## Brief and method

The references were judged against Jason's actual brief, not against a generic award-site rubric:

1. Can a recruiter or engineering leader identify the person, current role, and engineering value within roughly ten seconds?
2. Does the 390 x 844 first screen establish a deliberate hierarchy without hiding required content behind sideways discovery?
3. Is the personal identity specific enough to remember without overpowering the career story?
4. Are claims supported by employers, projects, outcomes, writing, or other inspectable evidence?
5. Is interaction purposeful, restrained, keyboard-compatible in principle, and avoidable under reduced motion?
6. Is the career chronology easy to scan rather than merely complete?
7. Is contact direct?
8. Could the useful pattern remain fast and practical on a static personal site?

Eight live sites were reviewed directly. All eight were visually inspected at a mobile viewport; Brittany Chiang, Maggie Appleton, and Rauno Freiberg were also inspected at 1280 x 800. The accepted Revision 53 yfyau.com preview was inspected at both responsive compositions as the control. This was a product-pattern study, not a lab performance benchmark or a claim that every reference has perfect accessibility.

## What the mature sites agree on

### 1. Identity is a sentence, not a decoration

The best opening can be repeated from memory: Brittany builds accessible, pixel-perfect experiences; Maggie makes visual essays; Anthony is an open sourceror and design engineer; Paco crafts interfaces. The visual system reinforces that sentence instead of substituting for it.

For Jason, `I build software that holds up.` is strong enough to keep because it is specific, compact, and consistent with the existing evidence. It should continue to sit beside the exact current role rather than float as an unsupported virtue claim.

### 2. Mature pages choose one primary proof system

- Brittany uses career chronology.
- Lee uses selected writing.
- Anthony uses recognizable open-source artifacts.
- Emil and Paco use small project indexes.
- Maggie uses a deep publishing archive.

Combining all five would make Jason's page less credible, not more complete. His strongest available proof system is career chronology plus attributed outcomes from prior roles.

### 3. Personality works when it is authored and bounded

Josh's interactions, Maggie's illustrated language, Rauno's canvas, and Anthony's playful title are not generic flourishes. Each grows from the person's actual work and voice. Jason's bee has the same potential because it comes from his name and has existed since the original site. Snowboarding and games are supporting biography; they should not compete with the bee for ownership of the interface.

### 4. Established reputation changes how much explanation a site needs

Lee, Emil, Paco, and Anthony can compress chronology because their companies, products, writing, and open-source work already carry context. That is useful editing inspiration, but not evidence that Jason should remove career detail wholesale. The more comparable reference is Brittany's explicit experience structure, shortened with Lee's discipline.

### 5. Sideways discovery is a deliberate cost

Rauno's entire canvas and Paco's compact columns make exploration part of the experience. That can be appropriate for design work, but it is the wrong cost for eight core skills or a short career proof set. Revision 53's choice to expose every strength without a scroll hint is reinforced by this study.

## Implications for the current Revision 53

### Preserve

- The generated bee as the single signature image and interaction.
- The exact name, current Okta role/date, and `I build software that holds up.` opening.
- The cream, ink, teal, and amber editorial palette and sharp-rule composition.
- The desktop split hero, which already combines career clarity with visual identity more effectively than most references.
- The career-first order, complete static strengths, attributed prior-role metrics, direct email, reduced-motion path, and restrained hobby placement.

### Reconsider in the next design round

- At 390 x 844, the current first screen ends inside the bee image. It communicates identity very well, but neither the direct email action nor an outcome proof point is visible yet.
- The full chronology is useful, but only the strongest prior roles need expanded outcome detail. Supporting roles should remain factual and compact.
- The bee interaction should remain the only major motion signature. Adding global cursor effects, multiple animated cards, sound, theme toys, or a terminal would weaken the career dossier.
- A Projects, Writing, or Garden section should not exist until Jason has a small set of public artifacts worth curating. Empty architecture is not maturity.

## Recommended design direction

### North star: career dossier with one living emblem

1. **Hero:** name, current role and date, durable engineering promise, bee, and one direct action or visible proof edge on mobile.
2. **Selected impact:** two or three attributed outcomes, with prior-company provenance visually inseparable from each metric.
3. **Experience:** current Okta role first without invented duties; two evidence-rich prior roles expanded; supporting history compact but complete.
4. **About:** the name-to-bee explanation, then one concise line each for snowboarding and games.
5. **Contact:** email and public GitHub, without a form or social-icon wall.

The visual north star is **Brittany's structure + Lee's editing + Maggie's authorship + one Rauno/Josh-level signature moment**. It is not a visual clone of any of them.

## Proposed next experiment, not yet authorized or implemented

Prototype only two 390 x 844 hero compositions while preserving the accepted desktop hero:

- **Proof peek:** shorten or recrop the mobile bee panel enough that the first outcome row or Email action is visibly beginning at the bottom of the first screen.
- **Action before image:** keep the large bee panel but place one direct action and one attributed proof line before it.

Compare them against the same five questions: Can a new visitor state Jason's name, current role, engineering promise, one proof point, and contact path after a short scan? Does the bee remain memorable? Is every required item available without sideways scrolling? No implementation should begin until that direction is reviewed.

## Limits

- This is a dated 2026-08-12 snapshot; personal sites change.

- The study judges fit for Jason's current brief. A lower fit is not a judgment that the reference site is poorly designed for its own purpose.
